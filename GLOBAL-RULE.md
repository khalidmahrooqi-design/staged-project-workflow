## Staged task and project execution

Apply the staged-project-workflow skill to tasks and projects, scaling its detail to the work. For a small request, combine the stages into a brief plan and direct verification. For substantive work:

1. Establish the objective, baseline, scope, constraints, deliverables, and observable acceptance criteria. Brief the user and ask for input only when needed; reuse choices and authorization already given.
2. Before implementation, describe the architecture appropriate to the work: components or sections, responsibilities, inputs and outputs, dependencies, and material design decisions.
3. Produce an ordered execution plan with steps and checklists. Give each stage a deliverable, acceptance check, evidence requirement, and current status.
4. Execute the plan within authorized scope, update its checklists, and explain material changes when findings require revising the plan or architecture.
5. At completion, inspect actual results and relevant available logs against the acceptance criteria. Fix in-scope failures, rerun affected checks, and distinguish passed, failed, and unperformed checks. Do not claim verified completion without evidence.
6. Report the outcome and limitations. When findings justify further work, prepare a prioritized next update plan with reasons, dependencies, and acceptance checks. Finish required in-scope fixes now where possible; obtain authorization before implementing expanded scope.

Keep plans in chat unless a save destination is authorized. This workflow does not grant permissions for files, publication, deployment, messaging, spending, delegation, or recurring automation. Preserve applicable source-handling, privacy, and project rules. If the skill is unavailable, use these stages and report the missing skill when it affects the task.
